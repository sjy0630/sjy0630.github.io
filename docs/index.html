01

About

I'm an undergraduate at the College of Integrated Circuits and Micro-Nano Electronics, Fudan University (class of 2025). Chips were what drew me in first. Over time I've become more interested in the whole path from an algorithm to silicon: how models turn into real computation, what system constraints limit their capabilities, and how to tell whether a result that looks good is actually trustworthy.

So far that has taken me through physical modeling of memristors, Transformer-based causal inference, and an agentic chip-design hackathon. Alongside coursework I've been working through MIT 6.004 (computer architecture) and MIT 6.5940 (efficient deep learning), which is where my current interest in AI accelerators and hardware–software co-design comes from.

This site isn't a finished résumé. It's a place to keep the parts of the process that stuck: a piece of code, an experiment, a question I didn't let myself gloss over.

03

Projects

  • VULCAN-2D ↗

    Research · open source

    A multi-scale physical modeling framework and simulator for 2D-material memristors, built as a Fudan Xiyuan undergraduate research project. It covers conductive-filament evolution, charge transport, Joule heating, and device-to-device variability, with numerical models and I–V simulation.

    The first time I really faced it: a model that fits the data hasn't necessarily explained the mechanism.

    • Memristors
    • Physical modeling
    • Simulation

  • Causal Transformer

    Research · in progress

    Research on whether Transformer models can learn latent causal rules from offline data and make their own experimental decisions. I run experiments in PyTorch — data generation, training, evaluation — and have analyzed distribution shift that appears once the model acts in a closed loop.

    No conclusions yet — but it's teaching me to separate “the code runs”, “a metric moved”, and evidence I can actually trust.

    • Causal inference
    • Active experiment design
    • PyTorch

  • Agentic4Systems Hackathon

    2026 · Third prize

    A four-day agentic AI chip-design hackathon. On a team of three, I was responsible for the C model, the synthesis flow, and part of the RTL changes.

    More than the ranking: ISA semantics, reference model, RTL, and PPA aren't separate boxes — they're one chain that has to be kept in sync.

    • C model
    • RTL
    • Synthesis
    • PPA
